How this works
  1. Each camera is a room. Frames go to MoveNet MultiPose (TensorFlow.js) running in your browser: up to 6 people, 17 keypoints each, with a built-in box tracker giving stable ids. In the Python backend this stage is YOLOv8-pose + ByteTrack on an RTSP feed; the classifiers downstream are the same.
  2. Per track, each frame yields cues: motion (box-widths/s), a nearby moving partner, relative velocity, sustained arm raise, nose below the shoulder line, foot point outside every seat zone.
  3. Each behavior sits behind a sustained-condition gate: fighting 3 s at ≥60 % of frames, sleeping 12 s at ≥85 %, out of seat 8 s at ≥80 %. A single frame can never alert.
  4. Repeats merge into the open alert; per-track (120 s) and per-room (30 s) cooldowns stop duplicates.
  5. On alert a clip is kept from a 10 s pre-roll ring buffer plus 10 s post-roll. Nothing is recorded continuously.
  6. A staff member must acknowledge then confirm or dismiss. Only a human confirm marks a clip actionable; the system takes no action itself. Retention: unreviewed 48 h, dismissed 24 h, confirmed 90 d.
  • Video never leaves the device. There is no server; clips live in page memory and vanish on reload.
  • Talking is not detected: from video alone at classroom distance it is low confidence and needs an audio channel.
  • Seat zones are saved per camera in this browser.

Alert queue

Alerts are suggestions from an automated detector. A staff member must acknowledge and review each one; nothing is flagged actionable until a person confirms it.
