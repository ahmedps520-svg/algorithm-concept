How this works
  1. Each camera is a room. Frames go to MoveNet MultiPose (TensorFlow.js) in your browser: up to 6 people, 17 keypoints each, with stable per-person ids. The Python backend uses YOLOv8-pose and ByteTrack on an RTSP feed; everything downstream is the same.
  2. Per track, each frame yields cues: torso motion, head motion, a nearby moving partner, relative velocity, sustained arm raise, head down (nose well below the shoulder line, or the face gone from view with the head at shoulder level) with an awake-veto when both eyes are plainly visible at normal head height, foot point outside every seat zone, lip movement.
  3. Each behavior sits behind a sustained-condition gate with a minimum duration and a minimum fraction of active frames. Demo timing: fighting 2 s, sleeping 10 s, out of seat 6 s. Classroom timing: 3 / 20 / 8 s. A single frame can never alert.
  4. Talking is off by default: from video alone it is not reliable enough to act on. Switched on, it needs a neighbour within reach and visible lip movement from MediaPipe Face Landmarker, which only resolves close to the camera. In a classroom this cue needs an audio channel.
  5. A seat zone is created automatically around anyone who stays put for a few seconds; you can also draw zones by hand.
  6. Repeats merge into the open alert; per-track (120 s) and per-room (30 s) cooldowns stop duplicates.
  7. On alert a clip is kept from a 10 s pre-roll ring buffer plus 10 s post-roll. Nothing is recorded continuously.
  8. Optionally a local vision-language model reviews a 6-frame contact sheet of each clip and gives an advisory verdict with a reason.
  9. A staff member must acknowledge then confirm or dismiss. Only a human confirm marks a clip actionable; the system takes no action itself. Retention: unreviewed 48 h, dismissed 24 h, confirmed 90 d.
  • Video never leaves the device. There is no server; clips live in page memory and vanish on reload.
  • Seat zones, recordings and the trained model are saved per camera in this browser.
